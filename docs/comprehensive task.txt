You are required to perform a full accounting data-mapping audit, correction, and stabilization across the Expenses, Chart of Accounts, General Ledger, Journal Entries, Trial Balance, and Reports modules.

Work as both an expert system auditor and an elite software engineer. Your objective is to ensure that all financial data is mapped accurately, posted correctly, reported once, and fully traceable from source transaction to final report. Do not guess. Analyze the existing implementation, database relationships, posting logic, account mappings, report queries, and UI dropdown sources before making changes.

This task must be handled carefully because it affects financial accuracy, audit readiness, and the credibility of the entire system.

## 1. Expense Category Source in `/expenses`

In `/expenses`, under **Create New Expense**, the field **Expense Category*** must only retrieve its list from `/chart-of-accounts`.

Specifically:

* The dropdown must only show accounts under the **Expenses** account group.
* Only accounts with the correct expense account code range, starting from **5000**, must be listed.
* The list shown in **Expense Category*** must exactly match the expense accounts defined under `/chart-of-accounts`.
* No hardcoded expense categories should exist.
* No categories should be fetched from outdated tables, duplicated category tables, seed data, or static arrays.
* If an expense account is added, edited, disabled, or removed in `/chart-of-accounts`, the `/expenses` category dropdown must reflect that accurately.
* The dropdown must display the correct account name, account code, and account ID internally.
* The selected expense category must post the transaction to the exact selected chart of account, not to a similar or duplicated account.

The `/expenses` module must be fully dependent on the official Chart of Accounts as the single source of truth.

## 2. Correct Expense Posting Logic

Every expense recorded in the system must accurately fall under its correct account.

When an expense is created:

* The system must post the expense to the selected account from the Chart of Accounts.
* The posting must use the correct `account_id`, not just the account name or code text.
* The expense must appear correctly in:

  * `/expenses`
  * `/general-ledger`
  * `/journal-entries`
  * `/trial-balance`
  * `/reports`
  * Profit and Loss Statement
  * Expense reports
  * Any dashboard or summary that uses expense data

There must be no duplicate posting, double counting, or incorrect parent-child aggregation.

Each transaction must have one clear source, one correct account mapping, and one correct ledger impact.

## 3. Salaries and Wages Account Enforcement

Salaries and wages must only be posted to:

**Account 5200 – Salaries & Wages**

No salary-related expense should be posted to any other duplicate or similar account, including but not limited to:

* 5230 — Salaries Expense
* 5110 — Salaries & Wages Expense
* Salary Expense
* Wages Expense
* Payroll Expense
* Staff Salaries
* Employee Wages
* Any other duplicate or incorrectly named salary account

You must audit the entire system and ensure that all salary, payroll, employee wage, and staff compensation transactions are mapped only to **5200 – Salaries & Wages**.

If duplicate salary-related accounts exist, they must be handled carefully:

* Do not delete accounts blindly if they contain historical data.
* First analyze their usage.
* Move, remap, or consolidate related transactions into Account 5200 where appropriate.
* Preserve audit trail and historical transaction integrity.
* Ensure future salary postings can only use Account 5200.
* Disable or merge duplicate salary-related accounts where required, depending on the system’s architecture.

The final system must have one authoritative salary account only: **5200 – Salaries & Wages**.

## 4. Full Chart of Accounts Audit and Cleanup

Analyze the entire `/chart-of-accounts` module.

The Chart of Accounts must be treated as the official accounting structure of the system. It must be clean, unique, accurate, and free from duplicated or overlapping accounts.

Perform a full audit for:

* Duplicate account names
* Duplicate account codes
* Similar accounts with different names but same purpose
* Accounts receiving the same data incorrectly
* Parent accounts incorrectly receiving direct transaction postings
* Child accounts and parent accounts both holding the same transaction data
* Incorrect account types
* Incorrect account code ranges
* Incorrect mapping between modules and accounts
* Expenses posted to assets, liabilities, equity, or revenue accounts
* Revenue posted to expense accounts
* Manual or system-generated accounts that conflict with official accounts
* Old seed accounts still being used accidentally
* Hidden, disabled, or legacy accounts still receiving transactions

Each account must hold only its own correct data.

There must be no scenario where one transaction is posted into multiple chart of accounts unless it is a proper double-entry journal transaction with clear debit and credit sides.

Parent accounts must not duplicate the value of their child accounts. Parent balances must be calculated from child accounts where applicable, not posted separately unless the system design explicitly allows direct posting and handles it correctly.

The General Ledger must become the central reliable source for all financial reporting.

## 7. Trial Balance Accuracy in `/trial-balance`

Perform an extensive audit and correction of `/trial-balance`.

The Trial Balance must be 100% accurate and must match the General Ledger.

Ensure that:

* All accounts are listed correctly.
* Debit and credit balances are calculated correctly.
* Parent and child accounts are handled correctly.
* No duplicated balances appear.
* No account balance is double-counted.
* Expense accounts appear on the correct side.

If the Trial Balance does not balance, identify the exact transaction, journal entry, account, or module causing the issue and fix the root cause.

## 9. Data Mapping and Source-of-Truth Rules

Before making changes, analyze how data currently flows across the system.

Map the flow from:

* `/chart-of-accounts`
* `/expenses`
* `/journal-entries`
* `/general-ledger`
* `/trial-balance`
* `/reports`
* Payroll or salaries module, if available
* Payment modules
* Invoice modules
* Purchase/procurement modules
* POS or sales modules
* Any module that posts financial transactions

Identify:

* Which tables store financial transactions
* Which tables store ledger entries
* Which tables store journal entries
* Which columns reference accounts
* Whether account references use account ID, account code, or account name
* Where duplicated account mappings exist
* Where static mappings or hardcoded accounts exist
* Where transaction duplication is happening
* Whether reports are pulling from source transactions, journal entries, ledger entries, or mixed sources

After analysis, enforce one clean accounting flow.

Recommended flow:

1. Chart of Accounts is the single source of truth for all account definitions.
2. Source modules create business transactions.
3. Business transactions generate balanced journal entries.
4. Journal entries generate or represent General Ledger postings.
5. Trial Balance is calculated from the General Ledger.
6. Reports are calculated from the General Ledger and official Chart of Accounts structure.

Avoid scattered and inconsistent calculations across different modules.
